# Tool Absorption Doctrine

IntegrateWise may run on many tools.  
But it must not become a bag of tools.  
External platforms are subordinate capability providers until their strongest affordances are absorbed into the Workbench.

---

## 1. Purpose

- Define how external tools influence the IntegrateWise product.
- Prevent the stack from becoming a loose federation of linked apps.
- Ensure that the best capabilities of the runtime are progressively absorbed into a single adaptive operational surface.

---

## 2. Core Principle

Every tool in the stack must be classified into one of three categories:

- Native Surface Capability
- Absorbed Capability Source
- External Specialist Surface

The goal is not immediate replacement of all tools.
The goal is progressive absorption of their highest-value interaction patterns into IntegrateWise.

---

## 3. Absorption Rule

Do not clone entire tools.
Do not iframe entire products and call it integration.
Do not force users to keep five specialist UIs open for one operational task.

Instead:

- identify the strongest affordance,
- identify which Workbench primitive should inherit it,
- integrate it into the Projection + Workbench model,
- retain the tool as a specialist backend or fallback surface until native capability matures.

Doctrine:
Absorb capabilities, not brands.
Absorb interaction value, not interface bulk.

Role in stack:

- Human Cognition Layer.

Native strengths:

- block-based composition,
- freeform canvases,
- spatial note-taking,
- fluid scratch-to-structure thinking,
- collaborative drafting.

What IntegrateWise should absorb:

- block-based operational canvases,
- freeform contextual drafting,
- mixed structured/unstructured work surfaces,
- spatial thinking surfaces for Personal View and pre-canonical work.

- Operational Canvas
- Personal View
- drafting overlays
- session workspaces

Doctrine:
Affine is the fluid cognition benchmark, not the final canonical truth surface.

## 5. Capability Adoption Model

A capability moves through four states:

1. External only
2. Integrated reference
3. Native-assisted
4. Native-primary

Example:

- Open WebUI begins as external only.
- Then IntegrateWise links entity context into it.
- Then IntegrateWise offers native contextual chat panels.
- Finally, the native Twin surface becomes primary and Open WebUI becomes fallback/specialist.

Doctrine:
Absorption is staged.
Replacement follows demonstrated native superiority, not ideology.

---

## 6. Tool Retention Rule

A tool remains primary if:

- it still delivers a better specialist workflow than the native surface,
- the native surface has not yet absorbed its critical affordances,
- removing it would increase user friction or weaken governance.

A tool becomes secondary when:

- its key affordances have been absorbed into the Workbench,
- users can complete the core loop natively,
- the external UI is now mainly for deep admin or specialist tasks.

---

## 7. Builder Interaction With Tool Doctrine

Builders must never ask:

- "How do we expose this tool?"

Builders must ask:

- "What human value does this tool currently provide?"
- "Which Workbench primitive should inherit that value?"
- "What is the smallest native surface that makes this capability operationally coherent?"

---

## 8. Final Law

The stack may remain plural.
The product experience may not.

IntegrateWise succeeds when:

- the best capabilities of the stack compound inside one adaptive Workbench,
- the user sees one operational story,
- and the tools beneath it become infrastructure, not fragmentation.
